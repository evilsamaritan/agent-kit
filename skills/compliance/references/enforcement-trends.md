## GDPR Enforcement

Aggregate statistics (cumulative fines, annual totals, breach notifications per day) change every year and differ by tracker. Do not quote totals from memory or from this file: take them from a named, dated source such as a regulator's annual report or a maintained enforcement tracker, and cite it.

### Notable fines (regulator decisions)

| Entity | Amount | Regulator and reason |
|--------|--------|--------|
| TikTok | 530M EUR | Irish DPC, May 2025: transfers of EEA user data to China (485M EUR, Art. 46(1)) and transparency (45M EUR, Art. 13(1)(f)) |
| Google | 325M EUR | CNIL, September 2025 (200M EUR Google LLC, 125M EUR Google Ireland): ads shown between Gmail emails without consent and cookies placed at account creation without valid consent. A French cookie and e-communications law decision, not a GDPR fine |
| SHEIN | 150M EUR | CNIL, September 2025: advertising cookies placed before any interaction with the banner. A French cookie-law decision, not a GDPR fine |

Civil damages are not regulator fines: in November 2025 a Madrid commercial court awarded Spanish media publishers about 479M EUR against Meta for unfair competition linked to GDPR breaches (not re-verified). Do not count it as a GDPR fine.

### Focus Areas

- **Dark patterns:** regulators actively test websites, not just respond to complaints
- **Cookie consent:** making rejection harder than acceptance = violation
- **AI processing:** EDPB Opinion 28/2024 says legitimate interest can apply to AI model development and deployment only when strictly necessary and the balancing test is passed (see ai-act-compliance.md)
- **Missing DPIAs:** a recurring finding where high-risk processing (health, large-scale monitoring) was not assessed first
- **Cross-sector expansion:** enforcement is not limited to large platforms; smaller organizations and non-tech sectors are fined too

### GDPR amendment proposals

The Commission has proposed GDPR and cookie-rule amendments as part of its simplification (omnibus) agenda, including changes to records-of-processing exemptions for smaller organizations and to cookie consent. Proposals change during negotiation: check the legislative status and the adopted text before treating any change as law.

## EU AI Act: Key Dates

The Digital Omnibus on AI was agreed by Council and Parliament negotiators on 7 May 2026, adopted by Parliament at first reading on 16 June 2026 and by the Council on 29 June 2026, and entered into force in July 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (reported as published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and in force from 27 July 2026). Check the consolidated text of the AI Act for current dates.

| Date | What applies |
|------|--------------|
| 1 Aug 2024 | AI Act entered into force |
| 2 Feb 2025 | Prohibited AI practices; AI literacy provisions (Art. 4; read the consolidated text, since the omnibus amended parts of the Act) |
| 2 Aug 2025 | General-purpose AI model obligations and governance rules |
| 2 Aug 2026 | Remaining general application date, including Art. 50 transparency, except where the omnibus defers a part |
| 2 Dec 2026 | Art. 50(2) marking of synthetic content for systems already on the market (grace period under the omnibus); new prohibitions on non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material |
| 2 Dec 2027 | High-risk systems listed in Annex III (employment, credit, education, biometrics, law enforcement and others), postponed from 2 Aug 2026 |
| 2 Aug 2028 | High-risk systems that are safety components of products under Annex I harmonization law, postponed from 2 Aug 2027 |

The adoption dates come from Council and Parliament announcements. The application dates in this table come from secondary summaries of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 that agree with each other; the official text on EUR-Lex was not readable in the October 2026 check, so confirm a date there before relying on it in advice.

### Penalty Structure

| Violation type | Maximum penalty |
|---------------|----------------|
| Prohibited AI practices | 35M EUR or 7% global turnover |
| High-risk non-compliance | 15M EUR or 3% global turnover |
| Incorrect information to authorities | 7.5M EUR or 1% global turnover |

## Cross-Border Transfer Developments (not re-verified unless a source is named)

- **US-EU Data Privacy Framework (DPF):** active, enables EU-US transfers for certified companies
- **UK adequacy:** the Commission renewed both UK adequacy decisions (GDPR and Law Enforcement Directive) on 19 December 2025; they run until 27 December 2031 (European Commission)
- **US DOJ bulk data rule (effective April 2025):** prohibits sharing American sensitive data with countries of concern (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela)
- **UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025:** introduces risk-based comparative assessment for transfers
- **EU Data Act (Sept 2025):** extends sovereignty to non-personal and industrial data
- **Vietnam Law on Data (2025):** government approval required for core data exports
- **China PIPL:** continues to require local storage with restricted cross-border transfers

## Children's Privacy (2025-2026)

- **FTC amended COPPA Rule:** published 22 April 2025, effective 23 June 2025; regulated entities had until 22 April 2026 to comply, except for some safe-harbor provisions (Federal Register 2025-05904). First major overhaul since 2013. ("COPPA 2.0" is a separate legislative proposal, not this rule.)
  - Separate consent for disclosing children's data to third parties and for advertising
  - Expanded personal information definition (biometric identifiers, government IDs)
  - Written information-security program and data retention policy requirements
- **FTC age verification policy (not re-verified):** confirm any safe-harbor claim for mixed-audience sites against the FTC's published text.
- **EU:** enforcement intensifying around children's data, especially in gaming and social media.

## Privacy Standards (not re-verified)

- **ISO 27701:2025 (Oct 2025):** major revision — now standalone (no ISO 27001 prerequisite), 3-year transition to Oct 2028
- **NIST Privacy Framework:** under review (April 2025), aligning with Cybersecurity Framework 2.0
- **Evidence-based accountability:** auditors and regulators now expect operational evidence (automated data maps, DPIAs, AI risk assessments), not just policies

---

## Trend Summary

1. Enforcement is broadening (beyond Big Tech) and accelerating
2. AI governance is the fastest-growing compliance domain
3. Data sovereignty is replacing borderless data flows as the default
4. Children's privacy regulations are expanding globally
5. Compliance is shifting from policy-based to evidence-based accountability
6. Cross-border transfer mechanisms are fragmenting by jurisdiction
